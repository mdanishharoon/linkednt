# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

General professionals who live in the LinkedIn feed and are quietly exhausted by it: the performative humble-brags, the "I'm humbled to announce," the AI-generated thought-leadership slop. They're online, scrolling, and primed to laugh in recognition. The landing page catches them in that mood and converts the laugh into a Chrome install. Their job-to-be-done is simple: "make this feed bearable, and make me feel sane for finding it ridiculous."

## Product Purpose

Linkednt is a Chrome extension that translates performative LinkedIn and AI-generated "slop" posts into an honest, plain-English version, inline in the feed. This Next.js app is its brand-first landing surface: the storefront that sells the extension. The product's entire value is honesty, so the site has to be the funniest, clearest demonstration of that promise. Success is installs and shares, the humor is the growth engine, so the page is judged on whether someone laughs and forwards it before they finish reading.

## Brand Personality

Deadpan and meta-ironic. Three words: deadpan, meta-ironic, honest. The voice weaponizes the exact AI-speak it exists to kill, turning the slop's own tics against it: "it's not X, it's Y," "let that sink in," "cold hard truth, no fluff," "thrilled to announce," "humbled." It deploys those patterns with a straight face, then lets them collapse under their own absurdity, never winking, never adding a "lol." The joke is the contrast between corporate AI performance and plain truth, delivered flat. Confidence over zaniness. The product practices what it preaches: when it drops the bit, its copy is the plain-English honesty it sells.

## Anti-references

- Not the generic AI-startup landing page: no purple gradients, no glassmorphism, no "hero metric" template, no gradient text. That cliche is exactly the slop the product exists to mock, and the user's hard "no."
- Not a _sincere_ LinkedIn clone. The uncanny-valley mimicry is a deliberate satirical device. If it reads as an earnest enterprise tool with no puncture, the joke is dead.
- Not zany or meme-spammy. Deadpan means composed. Loud randomness undercuts the dry, meta-ironic delivery.

## Design Principles

- **Show the translation, don't describe it.** The before/after de-slopping is the pitch. Lead with a real transformation, not a paragraph explaining the feature.
- **Deadpan, never zany.** The frame stays composed and straight-faced so the satire lands. Restraint is the punchline's setup.
- **The medium is the joke (uncanny valley).** The page mirrors LinkedIn's own UI closely enough to feel familiar, then breaks the spell with moments of mischief. Borrow LinkedIn's vocabulary and structure, then puncture it. It can look like the thing it mocks, as long as the puncture is unmistakable.
- **Honesty as feature.** Plain English is the whole product, so the site's own copy and UI must be the clearest, least performative version of themselves. No slop in the slop-killer.
- **Funny but legible.** Humor never costs readability or accessibility. If a joke hurts comprehension, the joke loses.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA baseline: sufficient color contrast, full keyboard navigation, and `prefers-reduced-motion` support. Comedy stays readable, type and contrast are never sacrificed for a visual gag, and any satirical UI mimicry remains usable by everyone.
